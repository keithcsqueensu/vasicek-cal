# Vasicek-rate maximum likelihood (M3)

This note describes, in plain prose, how vasicek-cal estimates PD and the asset correlation ρ of
the one-factor Vasicek model from a series of observed default *rates*, rather than counts. It is
the estimator for rate series (FRED, StatCan; D-046), and the large-portfolio approximation of the
binomial-mixture MLE (`binomial_mixture_mle.md`) for count data. Decision numbers (D-nnn) point to
`DECISIONS.md`.

The accompanying script `validation/scipy/vasicek_rate_mle.py` reproduces the method with ordinary
scipy tools (section 6).

## 1. Model and likelihood

The model is the binomial mixture's, with the portfolio taken as infinitely granular: the default
rate in period t is the conditional PD itself,

    R_t = Φ( (Φ⁻¹(PD) − √ρ · Z_t) / √(1 − ρ) ),   Z_t ~ N(0, 1) independent,

the same p(z) as in the binomial model, in the engine's convention (a higher Z means better
conditions). So x_t = Φ⁻¹(R_t) is normal with mean c/√(1 − ρ), c = Φ⁻¹(PD), and variance
ρ/(1 − ρ), and the log density of an observed rate r, with x = Φ⁻¹(r), is

    l_t(PD, ρ) = ½ log(1 − ρ) − ½ log ρ + x²/2 − (√(1 − ρ)·x − c)² / (2ρ).

The panel log-likelihood is ℓ = Σ_t l_t. The term x²/2 does not depend on the parameters; it is
kept so that log-likelihoods are the true values. There is no integral: each term is closed form,
computed in double with Φ⁻¹ from `core/special` (the complement is used for rates above ½, so a
rate near 1 keeps its digits).

For count data, the rate is r_t = d_t / n_t. Reading d/n as the conditional PD ignores the
binomial noise in d_t, which the rate model then takes for factor variance: ρ̂ is biased upwards
by an amount that falls like 1/n. When that matters is study S-27's question.

## 2. Rates of 0 and 1 (D-044)

A rate of exactly 0 or 1 has no density: Φ⁻¹ is infinite. The treatment is chosen explicitly, and
is part of the objective's type, so that the panel check can see it:

- **Refuse (parity):** the panel is refused, and `zero_rate_periods` names the periods. Nothing is
  edited silently.
- **Censor (`native`):** a true censored likelihood. A zero rate is read as a rate below a
  detection limit δ, and contributes the probability of that event, the Vasicek CDF, instead of a
  density: log P(R ≤ δ) = log Φ((√(1 − ρ)·Φ⁻¹(δ) − c)/√ρ), computed as log Φ directly. A rate of 1
  contributes log P(R ≥ 1 − δ) = log Φ((√(1 − ρ)·Φ⁻¹(δ) + c)/√ρ).
- **Substitute (`native`):** a continuity correction. A zero rate is replaced by δ (a rate of 1 by
  1 − δ) and enters the ordinary density. It is a data edit, stated as such.
- **Drop:** the periods are removed before the fit (`drop_zero_rate_periods`). Also a data edit,
  and never done implicitly.

For count data the detection limit is half a default, δ = 1/(2n_t). For a rate series without
counts the caller supplies it. Which treatment is preferable is study S-28's question.

## 3. Estimation

The rate objective runs through the same engine as the binomial one: the log-likelihood surface on
the grid, argmax and quadratic refinement, Hessian SEs, the profile-likelihood intervals of D-128
to D-130 (threshold 1.9207) and S-23's interval for the 99.9% conditional PD, whose code is generic
in the objective. The grid, box and flags are the same. The per-run quadrature check compares two
integrators; this objective has none, so the check passes trivially.

**The closed form.** Without rates of 0 or 1, the maximum is known exactly: x_t is normal, so the
MLE is the sample mean μ̂ and the 1/T variance v̂ of x_t, and by invariance

    ρ̂ = v̂ / (1 + v̂),   PD̂ = Φ( μ̂ / √(1 + v̂) ).

The engine does not use it: the grid path keeps one mechanism for every objective, including the
resampling of D-042, and handles the censored likelihood, which has no closed form. The closed form
is an independent check: on rate panels from the recovery DGP, the profile code's polished maximum
agrees with it within 5.4·10⁻⁸ in logit units, and its log-likelihood within 3.4·10⁻¹³
(`TOL_VASICEK_RATE_POLISH_U`). Where the closed form lies outside the box, the grid path reports
the box's constrained maximum and flags it, as for the binomial fit.

**Accuracy of the terms.** Against mpmath at 50 digits, every log density and every censored or
substituted boundary term is within 2.9 ε times the size of its terms (the terms are added in
double, and √(1 − ρ)·x − c can cancel). The tolerance is `TOL_VASICEK_RATE_LOGLIK_EPS`; the goldens
are generated by `tools/gen_vasicek_rate_goldens.py`.

## 4. Recovery on its own model: predictions made before the pinning run

The rate estimator is checked on data from its own model: the n → ∞ limit of the recovery panels.
For each (PD, ρ, T) of the recovery matrix (27 cells) the factor draws z_t of the n = 10⁴ recovery
scenario (same seed, scenario id and replicate) are mapped to rates through the model, using the
DGP's deterministic Φ and Φ⁻¹ so the panels are the same on every platform. R = 1,000 replicates;
coverage is judged against the band of D-131 (0.927–0.973). `rate_recovery_harness` writes the
summary (`tests/golden/vasicek_rate/recovery_summary.csv`) and replays it (`recovery_rate_replay`,
labelled slow).

**Written before the R = 1,000 run.** Seen beforehand: an R = 50 run used to time the harness.

- **Mechanism.** On its own model the rate MLE is the MLE of a normal mean and variance in x, so
  its small-sample behaviour is the textbook one: v̂ is biased low by the factor (T − 1)/T, so ρ̂ is
  biased low; and profile-likelihood intervals from a χ²₁ threshold undercover slightly at small T,
  as S-13 found for the binomial fit at T = 20 (about one point).
- **Predictions:**

  | # | Prediction |
  |---|---|
  | V1 | **Closed form:** the polished maximum is within `TOL_VASICEK_RATE_POLISH_U` of the closed form in every replicate where the latter is inside the box |
  | V2 | **T = 40 and 100:** the profile coverage of PD, ρ and q is in the band in at least 50 of the 54 verdicts (18 cells × 3) |
  | V3 | **T = 20:** the profile coverage is 0.925–0.955 in at least 24 of the 27 verdicts (9 cells × 3), and below 0.95 in at least 18 of them |
  | V4 | **Bias:** ρ̂'s mean error is negative in at least 25 of the 27 cells |
  | V5 | **Flags:** in every cell at most 1% of fits carry a Wald flag |
